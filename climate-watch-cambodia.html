      Sources

      • Fire detections: NASA FIRMS, MODIS Collection 6.1 active fire (MCD14ML), Terra and Aqua. DOI 10.5067/FIRMS/MODIS/MCD14ML. Downloaded by us from the FIRMS archive.
      • Instrument: MODIS, the Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer, flown on NASA's Terra and Aqua satellites. Its fire product flags 1 km pixels that are burning at the moment of overpass under relatively clear skies, so it counts hotspots seen, not area burned. Terra passes in the morning and Aqua in the early afternoon (about 10:30 and 13:30 local time), so the two see different fires. Collection 6.1 changed the calibration of the input imagery, not the detection logic. This is a direct satellite observation, not a model.
      • Province centres: NASA SEDAC GPWv4 administrative-unit centre points, as already stored in Earth Buddy's data/gazetteer.json. DOI 10.7927/H4BC3WMT. The five regions are our own grouping of those provinces, for readability.
      • Cross-check series: Earth Buddy's data/v/fire.json (Cambodia, 2001–2024).
      • Statistics: Mann–Kendall with tie correction, Sen slope, lag-1 autocorrelation with trend-free pre-whitening when needed, Pettitt step test. Implemented in this page's JavaScript and checked against the Python in fire-watch/tools/stats_tests.py on many windows.

      How much smoke-type aerosol is in the air, when, and does it follow fire?

All three come from NASA's MERRA-2 reanalysis (the Giovanni files already in Climate Watch), averaged over a box covering Cambodia and parts of Thailand and Vietnam, monthly from January 2001 to December 2024.               Statistical Methodology

              Mann–Kendall, Sen's Slope & Pettitt Test

              Climate data is skewed and non-normal. Non-parametric rank tests avoid false alarms (like the NASA FIRMS Aqua fire counts, p = 0.92) while catching true regime shifts (2017–2018).
